Northern Tanzania // Mt. Kilimanjaro Foothills

The Erangau Community Water Supply Project

In the Maasai community of Erangau, families have historically faced arduous water-hauling journeys of up to 10 kilometers during dry seasons. To address chronic seasonal shortages, EWB-SBV partnered with community leaders, the local water board, and the Longido Water District to design and construct a new potable water distribution system.

The system uses treated water from the Simba River in the northern foothills of Mt. Kilimanjaro. Phase 1 included a storage tank, 3.75 km of water pipe, a public tap stand, and a pressure-reducing tank.

Infrastructure Completed:

Central storage tank, pressure reducing tank, 3.75-kilometer transmission pipeline, and a central community tap stand.

Expedition 02

Currently in progress

Alta Verapaz, Guatemala // San Cristóbal Verapaz

Río Chixoy Water Supply Project

Situated in the Municipality of San Cristóbal Verapaz, the rural community of Río Chixoy is home to approximately 150 residents in 30 households whose livelihoods center on agriculture and local gypsum quarrying in steep mountain terrain.

The community has faced severe water insecurity, depending on an unreliable cross-river connection from a neighboring municipality that frequently cuts off service or provides water for only an hour or two per day. Water quality from this source is poor and requires boiling before use.

Working alongside the community’s elected COCODE (Community Development Council, which serves as the local Water Board), the chapter identified an abundant natural spring emerging from a limestone cave. Water quality testing coordinated with regional health authorities confirmed the spring is clean and free of biological and chemical contaminants.

Collaborative Framework:

A formal multi-party project agreement signed with municipal authorities, local leadership, and partner liaison establishes the cooperative path toward designing an independent gravity distribution network.

Methodology // Collaborative Engineering

How We Work

Sustainable water infrastructure succeeds when engineering rigor and local community leadership operate as equal partners.

01

Community Partnership

Projects begin with open dialogue. We collaborate directly with community leaders and water committees to understand local water needs, listen to community priorities, and establish mutual commitments from the outset.

02

Technical Assessment

Our volunteer engineers perform site investigations, collect design data, evaluate water sources, and coordinate water-quality testing to develop reliable water-system designs.

03

Hands-On Implementation

Construction brings together EWB-SBV, local partners, and community members to implement pipelines, storage tanks, tap stands, and related infrastructure.

04

Local Stewardship

We provide practical training to local caretakers and water committees on system operation, routine maintenance, and water treatment, ensuring long-term community ownership and independent operation.
